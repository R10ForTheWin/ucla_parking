Password Safety FAQ

Your questions answered

Is my UCLA password stored on the server?

No. Your credentials are saved only on your own phone — the same way Safari saves passwords. They are never written to any database or server.

It's sent over the internet when I tap Buy — can someone intercept it?

No. The connection uses HTTPS encryption, the same standard used by your bank and every major website. Your password is encrypted in transit.

Can the developer see my password?

No. Your password is used in the moment to log into the UCLA parking site and is never logged, recorded, or stored anywhere on the server.

What if the server gets hacked?

There's nothing to steal. Since credentials are never stored server-side, a hacker would find no passwords — only the app code itself.

What if someone steals my phone?

Anyone with access to your unlocked phone could open the app and see your saved password. This is the same risk as any password saved in Safari — use Face ID or a passcode to lock your phone.

Did Justin Timberlake really help develop this?

Yes, and now he lives inside your phone.

Why use this instead of the UCLA ePermit website?

Speed. The ePermit site requires many clicks, logins, and steps. Justin does all of that for you in the background — one tap and you're done. It's fast enough to purchase a permit at a red light on the way to class.

Is this AI powered?

No. Justin contains zero AI. Every step is explicit, hand-coded logic — it fills in your username and password, clicks the same buttons you would, and stops. There is no AI model involved at any point.

Couldn't AI go rogue and share my info?

That concern doesn't apply here — Justin is not AI. It's a simple automation script that follows a fixed set of instructions. It has no ability to make decisions, send data anywhere, or do anything other than exactly what it was programmed to do: buy your parking permit.
